01 — Growth

The business is growing

More revenue, more cities, and the mature markets are performing better than they ever have.

On pace to finish roughly 10 shows ahead of last year — without a normal-cadence Los Angeles, and with Philadelphia/Atlanta below their usual pace. Real room left even where I'm not adding cities.
02 — Where the money went

New-market investment, not cost creep

City count grew 23 → 26. New cities cost money before they pay off — that's venue fees, not overspending in markets I already run.

The Denver situation: the spring show got a sweetheart deal (forced venue change, fees waived). Fall went back to normal terms — $2,500 rental + $12,000 bar minimum — and we fell $9,845 short, settled in March 2026. That single item is a meaningful piece of why this year looks softer than it otherwise would.

06 — Forward

What I need going forward

Sell now

If you'd take something near what the independent valuations actually show — roughly $500K — I think we should start shopping it.

You take it back*

You resume full control starting January 1. I'd be interested in discussing continuing our working relationship in some form.

I stay as franchisee

Same agreement, but I start getting paid first, exactly as Section 4.1 already says.

*If we go this route, we'd want to work out a year-end close-out — making sure any expenses I've already covered are settled, and that any 2027 show costs I pay in advance get reimbursed within 30 days of the event.
